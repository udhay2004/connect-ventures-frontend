Why the right introduction matters more than the right address

Dr. Anil GuptaFounder and Chairman, Connect Ventures · 2 min read

Connecting is the C that money alone cannot buy: introductions to the distributors, agents, investors, bankers and officials who decide whether a newcomer to a market gets a meeting or a polite silence.

Researchers Jan Johanson and Jan-Erik Vahlne gave the problem a name in 2009: the liability of outsidership. In their revised Uppsala model, a firm outside the relevant business network struggles more than a firm that is merely foreign (Journal of International Business Studies, 2009). Small exporters say the same thing in surveys. Inability to contact overseas customers and difficulty finding reliable foreign representation both rank in their top five barriers (OECD–APEC, 2006).

Connect Ventures was a network before it was a firm. It grew out of a community of non-resident Indians and Indian entrepreneurs who wanted to do business with each other, and that habit still shapes the work. Connecting covers stakeholder search for distributors, C&F agents, warehouses, master franchisees and office space; investor introductions; chambers of commerce and trade bodies across more than 50 countries; and guidance on government relations and product certification.

We run partner searches as paid mandates: a small sign-up fee, with most of the fee payable on success. The structure keeps us honest. We earn most of our fee only when an introduction turns into a relationship.

The diaspora helps, but it is not a shortcut. About 3.73 crore Indians and people of Indian origin live overseas (MEA, January 2026), yet a 2025 study by IIFT economist V.C. Sabeer found that emigrants do not, by themselves, raise India's overall exports (reported by Outlook Business).

A shared surname opens a door. It does not close a deal.Dr. Anil Gupta

One practical test before any introduction: write a single page on why a foreign partner should want to work with you. If that page is hard to write, the introduction is premature.
