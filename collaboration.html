How cross-border partnerships hold up once the contract is signed

Dr. Anil GuptaFounder and Chairman, Connect Ventures · 2 min read

Collaborating is the C where introductions become daily operations: goods cleared through customs and held in a local warehouse, staff hired without a local entity, and agreements that spell out what each side owes the other.

The risk now shifts from strategy to execution. One wrong HS code changes the duty on every shipment that follows. An importer of record who does not know marketplace rules can leave inventory stuck outside a fulfilment centre. A distribution agreement signed without exit terms becomes expensive to unwind.

That last risk is well documented. David Arnold of Harvard Business School followed eight companies into almost 250 new country markets and found a recurring pattern: sales through a local distributor start strong, then plateau, and the exporter ends up making a costly switch to direct presence (summary of Arnold, Harvard Business Review, 2000). His advice was to plan the relationship from the start, not to avoid distributors.

Under the 5Cs, Collaborating covers importer, employer and agent of record services through vetted partners, including for sellers on Amazon FBA and Walmart; 3PL warehousing, customs brokerage and HS classification; deal documents such as term sheets, MOUs and shareholder agreements; and due diligence on a partner before anyone signs.

A collaboration gets documented before it gets celebrated.Dr. Anil Gupta

Every arrangement we set up starts with a written split of duties and costs, and a clause on how either party can leave. The exit clause matters most, because the partner who is right for year one is often wrong for year five.

The research rewards this discipline. Firms with a dedicated function for managing alliances reported 63 per cent of their alliances as successful, against a 50 per cent long-term success rate for firms without one (Kale, Dyer and Singh, 2002). Partnership is a skill that improves with a method.

What C4 Collaborating covers

IOR / EOR / AOR services

Importer of Record: ship to Amazon FBA USA, Walmart, UK, UAE, Singapore without a foreign entity. 15–20 vetted US IOR partners. Cost: 2–5% of CIF. Employer of Record: hire globally without a local entity. Agent of Record: regulatory submissions for pharma, electronics, medical devices.

Deal facilitation and documentation

SHA and term sheet drafting, MOU preparation, JV agreement structuring, due diligence coordination, and arbitration/mediation services for cross-border deals between Indian and foreign parties.

3PL warehousing and logistics

Third-party logistics warehousing in USA, UK, UAE, Singapore for Indian exporters. Pick, pack, ship fulfilment. Amazon FBA prep, labelling, and compliance bundled in.

Customs and EXIM services

Export/import documentation, HS code classification, duty calculation, customs clearance in USA/UK/UAE, origin certificates, and DGFT compliance for Indian exporters.

Company as a service / local representation

Provide an Indian business with a legal, operational presence in a foreign market through a local representative entity — without forming a new company. Useful for initial market testing.

Overseas assets and real estate

Overseas property acquisition advisory, cross-border asset structuring, estate planning for Indian HNIs with foreign assets, and repatriation of overseas proceeds.
