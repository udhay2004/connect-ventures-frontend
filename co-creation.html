When an adviser becomes a co-owner of the venture

Dr. Anil GuptaFounder and Chairman, Connect Ventures · 2 min read

Co-creation is the deepest of the 5Cs: for selected clients, Connect Ventures moves from adviser to partner, co-investing in or co-structuring a venture so that both sides share the risk and the reward of a foreign market.

It is not for everyone, and it should not be. Co-creation fits a business with a proven product at home, a target market already tested through the first four Cs, and a gap that capital, shared ownership or long-term operating support would close. A business still searching for its first overseas customer needs Coaching, not a co-investor.

In practice, Co-creation covers direct co-investment and syndicate structuring, joint venture design, cross-border M&A including company buy-and-sell, and access to commodity trading channels. Over time it extends to the operating systems, marketing networks and capital a venture needs to scale.

Joint ventures carry a mixed record, and anyone who sells them as easy is selling something. Writing in the Harvard Business Review, three McKinsey consultants judged JV success rates to be on a par with mergers and acquisitions, “not very good”, and blamed weak planning of the launch period that runs from the memorandum of understanding to the first 100 days (Bamford, Ernst and Fubini, 2004). Their fix is unglamorous: align the parents' strategies and settle governance before launch.

We apply the same discipline. Governance, decision rights and exit routes are written before capital moves. On the India side, the investment is structured to meet FEMA overseas investment rules from day one, because a co-created venture that cannot report cleanly to the RBI is a liability for both partners.

An adviser paid by the hour has no stake in whether the venture is alive in year five. A co-owner does.Dr. Anil Gupta

The logic behind co-creation is alignment, which is why we offer it rarely and only after the evidence says the market is real.
