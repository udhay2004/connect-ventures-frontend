Why global expansion starts with the founder, not the filing

Dr. Anil GuptaFounder and Chairman, Connect Ventures · 2 min read

Coaching is the first of the 5Cs because the most expensive decisions in a global expansion are made before any company is registered: which country, which customer, which version of the product, and how much of the founder's time the venture will take.

Most Indian founders reverse that order. They book a Delaware or Dubai incorporation, open a bank account, and only then ask who will buy. The new entity starts costing money from day one, in annual filings on both sides of the border, and the strategy gets written afterwards to justify it. Dr. Anil Gupta puts the warning to clients plainly: build operations that hold true substance, not entities that exist only on paper.

The odds are unforgiving. World Bank data covering dozens of countries show that only 43 per cent of new exporters survive beyond their first year (World Bank, 2018). When the OECD and APEC asked small exporters what held them back, identifying opportunities, finding market information and lack of management time all ranked in the top six (OECD–APEC, 2006). Those are thinking problems, not filing problems.

Think first, file second.Dr. Anil Gupta

Coaching at Connect Ventures works on them with the founder and the leadership team. It covers market selection and country prioritisation, positioning and investor messaging, localisation of product and go-to-market, and succession planning for family businesses. Country, function and regulatory coaches add the local detail of how deals actually get done.

The method has evidence behind it. A meta-analysis of 37 randomised trials found a moderate, consistent effect from coaching (de Haan and Nilsson, 2023).

The output is a market-entry map the founder owns. Sometimes it says “not yet”, and a good coach says so before the incorporation fee is paid.

Dr. Gupta has coached more than 8,000 entrepreneurs, and the order he teaches never changes: think first, file second.
